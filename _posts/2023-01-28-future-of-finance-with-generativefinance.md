I created [GenerativeFinance (GEFI.io)](https://gefi.io) as a place to explore the relationship between machine learning and financial services. The central question is practical: where can these techniques improve how we investigate a financial problem, and where do they simply make an uncertain answer look more convincing?

This article records the project's direction when I introduced it in 2023.

## What is Generative Finance?

I use *generative finance* here as a research theme, rather than a single established method. It brings together work on generating financial scenarios, constructing synthetic datasets, exploring strategies, and evaluating models that support financial decisions.

A generative model learns patterns from data and produces new samples. A forecasting model estimates an outcome of interest. These functions can overlap, but generating plausible market data is not the same as predicting the next market movement. That distinction should remain visible in any experiment.

## The Potential of [Generative Finance](https://gefi.io)

The attraction is the ability to investigate more possibilities: alternative market paths, changes in portfolio exposures, or conditions under which a strategy fails. The risk is mistaking a model's output for evidence about the world.

An experiment needs a comparison. Does the model improve on a simple baseline? Does the improvement survive transaction costs and data held out from training? Does it remain useful when the market behaves differently from the period used to build it? These are better questions than whether the output looks sophisticated.

## Key Topics Explored on [GEFI.io](https://gefi.io)

The questions I want this project to address include:

- **Scenario generation:** how to examine a range of outcomes without presenting simulated paths as forecasts.
- **Synthetic data:** what useful structure is preserved, what is distorted, and whether sensitive information could still be exposed.
- **Strategy evaluation:** how to separate a repeatable idea from a result produced by overfitting or favorable assumptions.
- **Risk communication:** how to explain uncertainty, model limitations, and the conditions under which a result should not be used.

My article on [Monte Carlo simulation in traditional and decentralized finance](/monte-carlo-simulation-finance/) develops one part of that agenda: using simulations to examine uncertainty while remaining explicit about the assumptions.

## Join Our Community

GEFI.io began with an invitation to researchers, practitioners, and people learning the field. The kind of contribution I value makes its method inspectable: a clear question, a documented dataset, a reproducible experiment, and an honest account of what did not work.

That is the standard I want to bring to the conversation around [GenerativeFinance](https://gefi.io/).
