The GO Raiding project began with a concern about who could continue participating in Pokémon GO after the April 2023 remote-raid changes. The responses strengthened my view that access deserves a more explicit place in game policy, alongside engagement and revenue.

The [survey archive](https://goraiding.com) includes player ratings, comments and the project’s methodology. These are responses from a voluntary sample, not a vote by the entire player base. They can identify problems worth investigating without establishing how common each problem is among all players.

## What the feedback should change

Remote participation can matter to people without a nearby raid community, players with mobility constraints and those balancing play with childcare. A design that works well for a group in a walkable city may create barriers for someone in a different situation.

My recommendation is to examine those situations directly. Invite affected players into research, describe the tradeoffs being tested and evaluate whether proposed alternatives preserve a practical way to take part. Rewarding local play and maintaining remote access need to be considered together.

## Measure the environmental question

The original concern was that some players would drive between raids when remote options became less useful. That is a plausible behavior to investigate, not a measured carbon outcome of this survey.

A stronger study would record travel mode, distance, occupancy and whether the trip was made specifically for the game. It would also compare periods and account for players who walked more, combined trips or stopped raiding. Concern about sustainability and evidence of changed emissions are different findings.

## Conclusion

Niantic’s [2023 announcement](https://pokemongo.com/news/remote-raid-passes-update-2023) explained its preference for in-person experiences. Players also deserve a clear account of how accessibility concerns influence that goal. Publish what has been learned, test alternatives and explain which evidence would justify changing the policy.

The case for a more inclusive game rests on understanding the constraints people actually face. The GO Raiding report was intended to make those constraints part of the conversation.
