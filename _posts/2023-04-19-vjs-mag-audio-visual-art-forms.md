I started [VJs Mag](https://vjsmag.com) in 2013 to give audiovisual work an editorial home: somewhere to discuss the people, techniques and ideas behind performances and installations. The publication paused in 2017 and returned in 2022. This note sets out the direction I was developing for it in 2023.

## A place to discuss audiovisual practice {#an-evolving-digital-hub}

The aim was to connect artists and audiences through work that often sits between established categories. A VJ performance involves music, moving image, software, hardware and the conditions of a live space. An installation may involve the visitor as an active participant. Covering those practices well means explaining the relationships between their parts.

I wanted the publication to make that exchange easier. Readers should be able to discover a project, understand how it works and find the people responsible for it.

## The editorial direction {#curated-content-for-a-dynamic-audience}

The editorial direction brought together artist profiles, project coverage and discussion of tools. Planned interviews would give practitioners room to explain their decisions, including the constraints and experiments that rarely appear in a finished showreel.

Technology coverage needed a similar level of specificity. A useful review would explain the setting in which a tool was tested, what it enabled and where it introduced friction. A list of features or a statement that something is innovative does little to help an artist decide whether it fits their practice.

Emerging artists also belonged in that conversation. Showing their work alongside its process could offer readers a more varied view of how an audiovisual practice develops.

## An editorial purpose {#be-part-of-the-thrilling-adventure-of-audio-visual-exploration}

The central ambition was straightforward: make audiovisual work easier to discover and discuss on its own terms. A publication can help by preserving credits, asking useful questions and giving each project enough context to be understood.

[VJs Mag](https://vjsmag.com) grew out of my interest in that exchange. This project history records the direction of the publication at the time, including ideas that were still plans rather than completed features.
